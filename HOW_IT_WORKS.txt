HOW IT WORKS — Patient-Aware Clinical Evidence MCP
====================================================

THE BIG PICTURE
---------------
A doctor asks a clinical question in Prompt Opinion.
Instead of getting a generic answer, the AI looks up the ACTUAL patient's
data and finds REAL published medical research before answering.


STEP-BY-STEP FLOW
-----------------

1. DOCTOR ASKS A QUESTION
   "Should this patient start anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation?"

2. PROMPT OPINION READS THE PATIENT CONTEXT (FHIR/SHARP)
   Prompt Opinion automatically knows which patient is active (Jane Doe).
   It forwards her patient ID to every tool call — this is called SHARP.
   Jane Doe's data lives in a FHIR store (standard healthcare data format).

3. ORCHESTRATOR DECIDES WHAT TO DO
   The Prompt Opinion AI (Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite) sees the question
   and decides to call our MCP tool: answer_clinical_question

4. OUR TOOL FETCHES THE PATIENT PROFILE  [get_patient_context]
   Using Jane Doe's patient ID, the tool retrieves:
   - Age, sex, weight
   - Active conditions (AFib, CKD stage 3b, Diabetes, Hypertension)
   - Current medications (Metformin, Amiodarone, Lisinopril, Atorvastatin)
   - Recent lab results (eGFR 38, Creatinine 1.8, HbA1c 7.8, INR 1.1)
   - Allergies (Penicillin, Sulfa drugs)

5. THE QUESTION IS REFORMULATED FOR PUBMED  [Gemini inside the tool]
   The raw question gets rewritten as a medical search query.
   Example: "atrial fibrillation anticoagulation CKD elderly DOAC"
   This is based on the PICO framework (Population, Intervention,
   Comparison, Outcome) used by clinical librarians.

6. PUBMED IS SEARCHED FOR REAL STUDIES  [search_pubmed]
   The tool queries PubMed (the world's largest medical research database)
   using the reformulated query and fetches up to 8 peer-reviewed articles.
   Each article has a title, abstract, journal, year, and PMID.

7. THE ANSWER IS SYNTHESIZED FOR THIS SPECIFIC PATIENT  [Gemini inside the tool]
   Gemini reads the research articles AND Jane Doe's profile together.
   It answers the question specifically for her — for example:
   - Her eGFR of 38 means standard DOAC doses need adjustment
   - Her weight of 52 kg triggers apixaban dose reduction criteria
   - Her amiodarone raises DOAC blood levels (P-gp inhibition)
   - Her CHA2DS2-VASc score of 5 means anticoagulation is strongly indicated
   Every claim is tied to a real PubMed article ID (PMID).

8. THE ANSWER IS RETURNED WITH AN EVIDENCE GRADE
   The final answer includes:
   - A patient-specific recommendation with citations [1][2][3]
   - An evidence grade: HIGH / MODERATE / LOW / INSUFFICIENT
   - Patient relevance notes explaining how her data changes the answer
   - A disclaimer that this is decision support, not a clinical decision


WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM JUST ASKING AN AI
---------------------------------------------
Generic AI:  "For atrial fibrillation, apixaban 5mg twice daily is typical."

Our tool:    "For Jane Doe specifically — given her weight (52 kg) and
              creatinine (1.8 mg/dL), she meets 2 of 3 dose-reduction
              criteria, so apixaban 2.5mg BID is the correct dose.
              Her amiodarone further supports this choice over other DOACs
              due to P-gp inhibition raising plasma levels. [PMID 38033089]"

The answer changes based on the actual patient. That is the point.


THE FILES THAT MAKE THIS WORK
------------------------------
main.py                  — Starts the MCP server, declares FHIR scopes
tools/fhir_patient.py    — Fetches patient data from FHIR (or demo patient)
tools/clinical_qa.py     — The main tool: PICO + PubMed + synthesis in one call
tools/pubmed_search.py   — Queries PubMed E-utilities API
tools/synthesize.py      — Standalone synthesis tool (used separately if needed)
tools/arxiv_search.py    — Searches arXiv for preprints (bonus tool)
jane_doe_fhir_bundle.json — Synthetic patient data used for the demo


DEMO PATIENT — JANE DOE (SYNTHETIC)
-------------------------------------
76-year-old woman, Newark NJ
New-onset Atrial Fibrillation (April 2026)
Chronic Kidney Disease Stage 3b — eGFR 38 (LOW)
Type 2 Diabetes — HbA1c 7.8% (HIGH)
Essential Hypertension

Medications: Metformin, Amiodarone, Lisinopril, Atorvastatin
Allergies: Penicillin (anaphylaxis), Sulfa drugs (rash)

This profile was designed to be clinically complex so that the tool
produces meaningfully different answers than generic AI would.
